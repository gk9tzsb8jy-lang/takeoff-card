N600NJ Takeoff Card

The takeoff, ground roll, accelerate-stop and landing charts from the Aerostar 600 FAA-approved Airplane Flight Manual, report VB-1201, Section 8 (Figures 8-3 to 8-8 and 8-21 to 8-24, knots and °C) were digitized and are run here the way you’d trace them by hand. Weight limits come from N600NJ’s gross weight supplement (AFMS ER94105, STC SA00266DE). Use it alongside the AFM, not instead of it.

Arms come from TCDS A17WE and the Owner's Manual Section 5. Limits come from TCDS A17WE and AFMS ER94105 (5,700 lb). Defaults are N600NJ's weight and balance of March 28, 2022. Use it alongside the manual, not instead of it.

    Checked against the AFM's examples

    The AFM works one example through every chart (Section 8, pages 8-4 to 8-16): takeoff at 20°C, 2,000 ft, 5,000 lb and a 10 kt headwind; landing at 15°C, 3,000 ft, 4,440 lb and a 10 kt headwind. These are the calculator’s answers for the same conditions. The AFM reads its answers off the chart by eye, so 1 to 2% either way is the reading accuracy of the chart itself.

    Chart conditions and limits

    • Level, paved, dry runway. Takeoff: 2575 RPM and full throttle before brake release, gear retraction started at or after the 50 ft obstacle. Accelerate-stop: engine failure at the engine failure speed, then idle power and maximum effective braking. Landing: maximum effective braking.
    • Each chart is traced on its own curves: OAT (°C) to the pressure altitude curve (sea level to 10,000 ft), along the weight guide lines from the 5,500 lb reference line, then along the headwind (to 30 kt) or tailwind (to 10 kt) guide lines. OAT past the end of a pressure altitude curve is flagged.
    • Ground roll comes from the AFM’s own ground roll charts (Figures 8-4, 8-6, 8-22 and 8-24), not a fixed fraction of the total.
    • Accelerate-stop uses the chart for the flap setting chosen (Figure 8-7 for flaps 20°, 8-8 for flaps up).
    • Charts stop at 5,500 lb. AFMS ER94105 raises N600NJ’s maximum takeoff and landing weight to 5,700 lb but gives no performance data, so from 5,500 to 5,700 lb the card extends the weight guide lines and flags the result.
    • Speeds are the AFM’s KIAS schedules, interpolated by weight and rounded up. Flaps 20°: rotate 85, 50 ft 89, engine failure 85 KIAS at 5,500 lb and less. Flaps up: rotate 85/88/92, 50 ft 90/93/96, engine failure 85/88/92 KIAS at 4,700/5,000/5,500 lb. Approach flaps 45°: 85/87/90 KIAS at 4,880/5,000/5,500 lb; flaps up 94/98/102/107 KIAS at 4,000/4,500/5,000/5,500 lb. Above 5,500 lb the 5,500 lb speed is shown.
    • Each chart was checked against the AFM’s worked example and agrees within about 1% (table on the left). Where the trace came out shorter than the book’s answer, that chart’s distances are scaled up by the difference (at most 1.5%), so errors lean long.
    • Off-station runways (airports with a paved runway of 2,800 ft or more): US airports come entirely from the FAA's 28-day airport data, refreshed weekly, including declared distances (TORA, ASDA, LDA). Airports outside the US come from OurAirports. Where no declared distances are published, takeoff uses full runway length and landing uses length minus any displaced threshold. Headings and winds are true, matching METARs; ATIS winds are magnetic.
    • KSPG uses the declared distances: TORA for the over-50 ft distance, ASDA for accelerate-stop, LDA for landing. FAA 28-day data, cycle effective October 1, 2026, updated from the runway database each cycle. Verify against the current Chart Supplement and NOTAMs.
    • Chart values are unfactored. The card flags any input outside the chart limits.

    Empty weight and oil

    Defaults are N600NJ's weight and balance of March 28, 2022 (3,912.9 lb at 165.62 in, moment 648,045). The TCDS empty weight includes only 7.5 lb of undrainable oil, so the card adds the other 37.5 lb of the 24 quarts at 145 in. Set it to 0 if that weight and balance was done with full oil.

      Limits used

      • Maximum ramp 5,725 lb, takeoff 5,700 lb, landing 5,700 lb (AFMS ER94105, STC SA00266DE). No zero fuel limit is published for the 600.
      • Forward CG limit 157.66 in up to 4,600 lb, 159.58 in at 5,500 lb, 160.03 in at 5,700 lb and 160.08 in at 5,725 lb, straight line between. Aft limit 167.88 in at all weights (TCDS A17WE, AFMS ER94105).
      • Arms: pilot and copilot 96 in, middle seats 132 in, rear seats 165 in (a rear bench takes three people up to 380 lb), baggage 245 in (TCDS; the 1976 manual's table uses 250), oil 145 in, wing fuel 170 in, fuselage fuel 213 in (from the manual's loading tables).
      • Start, taxi and runup: 25 lb, the gap between the ramp and takeoff limits.
      • Fuel burn follows the fuel system: all three tanks seek a common level, and the wings run dry with about 14 gal left in the fuselage tank (AOA training manual, 12 to 16 gal), after which the fuselage tank feeds alone. The burn line is checked every 50 lb, because the forward limit moves with weight.
