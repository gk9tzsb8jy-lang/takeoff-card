N600NJ Takeoff Card

The takeoff, accelerate-stop and landing charts from N600NJ's 1976 Owner's Manual (Figures 7-3, 7-4, 7-5 and 7-12) were digitized and are run here the way you'd trace them by hand. Weight limits come from N600NJ's gross weight supplement (AFMS ER94105, STC SA00266DE). Use it alongside the manual, not instead of it.

Arms come from TCDS A17WE and the Owner's Manual Section 5. Limits come from TCDS A17WE and AFMS ER94105 (5,700 lb). Defaults are N600NJ's weight and balance of March 28, 2022. Use it alongside the manual, not instead of it.

    Chart conditions and limits

    • Level, hard surface runway. Takeoff at maximum power. Landing with 45° flaps, power for a 3° approach and heavy braking after touchdown.
    • OAT 0 to 125°F (landing 0 to 120°F). Pressure altitude sea level to 10,000 ft (accelerate-stop to 8,000 ft). Headwind to 30 kt. Takeoff and landing charts show tailwind to 5 kt; the accelerate-stop chart shows none, so a tailwind there is applied with the flaps-up takeoff chart's tailwind ratio and flagged.
    • The charts give total distance over 50 ft. Ground roll is from the chart notes: 78% of total for flaps up, 80% at 4,000 lb to 72% at 5,500 lb for flaps 20°, and about 37% for landing.
    • Accelerate-stop is the flaps-up chart (engine failure at takeoff speed, heavy braking). The manual says the distance is shorter with 20° flaps, so it is used for both flap settings.
    • Charts cover 4,000 to 5,500 lb. AFMS ER94105 raises N600NJ's maximum takeoff and landing weight to 5,700 lb but gives no performance data, so from 5,500 to 5,700 lb the card extends the 5,000 and 5,500 lb lines and flags the result as extrapolated.
    • Speeds are the chart's mph IAS, interpolated by weight and converted to KIAS (rounded up), since N600NJ's airspeed indicator reads knots. Takeoff speed flaps up 94/97/102/107 mph (82/85/89/93 KIAS) and flaps 20° 92/93/94/94.5 mph (80/81/82/83 KIAS) at 4,000/4,500/5,000/5,500 lb; approach 94/100/105/110 mph (82/87/92/96 KIAS).
    • Off-station runways (airports with a paved runway of 2,800 ft or more): US airports come entirely from the FAA's 28-day airport data, refreshed weekly, including declared distances (TORA, ASDA, LDA). Airports outside the US come from OurAirports. Where no declared distances are published, takeoff uses full runway length and landing uses length minus any displaced threshold. Headings and winds are true, matching METARs; ATIS winds are magnetic.
    • KSPG uses the declared distances: TORA for the over-50 ft distance, ASDA for accelerate-stop, LDA for landing. FAA 28-day data, cycle effective October 1, 2026, updated from the runway database each cycle. Verify against the current Chart Supplement and NOTAMs.
    • Chart values are unfactored. The card flags any input outside the chart limits.

    Empty weight and oil

    Defaults are N600NJ's weight and balance of March 28, 2022 (3,912.9 lb at 165.62 in, moment 648,045). The TCDS empty weight includes only 7.5 lb of undrainable oil, so the card adds the other 37.5 lb of the 24 quarts at 145 in. Set it to 0 if that weight and balance was done with full oil.

      Limits used

      • Maximum ramp 5,725 lb, takeoff 5,700 lb, landing 5,700 lb (AFMS ER94105, STC SA00266DE). No zero fuel limit is published for the 600.
      • Forward CG limit 157.66 in up to 4,600 lb, 159.58 in at 5,500 lb, 160.03 in at 5,700 lb and 160.08 in at 5,725 lb, straight line between. Aft limit 167.88 in at all weights (TCDS A17WE, AFMS ER94105).
      • Arms: pilot and copilot 96 in, middle seats 132 in, rear seats 165 in (a rear bench takes three people up to 380 lb), baggage 245 in (TCDS; the 1976 manual's table uses 250), oil 145 in, wing fuel 170 in, fuselage fuel 213 in (from the manual's loading tables).
      • Start, taxi and runup: 25 lb, the gap between the ramp and takeoff limits.
      • Fuel burn follows the fuel system: all three tanks seek a common level, and the wings run dry with about 14 gal left in the fuselage tank (AOA training manual, 12 to 16 gal), after which the fuselage tank feeds alone. The burn line is checked every 50 lb, because the forward limit moves with weight.
